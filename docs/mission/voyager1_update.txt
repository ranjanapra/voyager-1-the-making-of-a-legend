 BULLETIN !!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!

Although many users need heliographic spacecraft coordinates with latitudes
calculated with respect to the solar equator, some may not want Carrington
longitudes which are in a system fixed to the rotating Sun. Inertial
longitudes are defined with respect to the ascending node of the solar equator
on the Ecliptic; this node was at solar ecliptic longitude +74.367 degrees
as of 1 Jan. 1900 at 1200 UT and its longitude increases by 1.4 degrees per
century due to precession of the equinoxes in the solar ecliptic system. 

Heliographic inertial longitudes have been added to the trajectory data files 
in COHO for all missions except Helios 1/2 and Pioneer Venus Orbiter; these 
latter missions will be done later.

 BULLETIN !!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!!

 The Voyager 1/2 plasma experiment P.I., Prof. J. Belcher of MIT, advises that
 recent analysis of the data finds instances of undersampling in low density
 regions associated with high speed solar wind streams, particularly in
 Voyager 2 data after 1989. All the Voyager 1 and 2 data are currently
 being reviewed and the NSSDC archived datasets will be updated in the
 near future. Until then, the Voyager plasma data should be used cautiously,
 particularly in regard to analysis of solar cycle effects, and it would
 be advisable to contact Prof. Belcher before publication of any analysis
 using data currently in COHO or in the NSSDC tape archive. See the *_FMT.txt
 files in VOYAGER1/PLASMA/HOUR and VOYAGER2/... for his direct statement.
 {These data were later superceded in 1995}
